I read your paper and your update switching to direct `2^(p+m)-1` candidates. I would be interested in a joint, reproducible experiment. I work with Crystal and maintain [cogni-ml](https://github.com/skuznetsov/cogni-ml), whose Metal infrastructure and CUDA Driver API layer we could reuse.

I built a [small-factor screening prototype](https://github.com/skuznetsov/mersenne-crystal-lab) specifically for your current odd-exponent stream. For each trial prime d, it computes `r=ord_d(2)` once and marks exponents satisfying `r|q`, without constructing the Mersenne integers. Keeping only progressions that can change the first-factor result reduces 6,541 trial primes through 65,521 to 479 retained progressions. CPU and Metal preserve factor precedence and proper-divisor semantics, with checked integer bounds and batches up to 16,777,216 candidates.

On an Apple M2 Max, I ran the bounded small-factor sieve over a billion odd exponents three times. Every CPU/Metal factor entry matched. Median filtering times were 3.33 seconds on the CPU and 2.83 seconds on Metal, including allocation, transfers, synchronization and output copying. These exclude final primality tests; 241,337,372 candidates remained unresolved after small-factor screening. The repository includes 55 passing CPU tests, 611 direct-exponent Metal comparisons against independent arithmetic, and an independently generated minimal-order oracle for all 6,541 primes.

I also added a reusable multicore GMP worker pool and a parallelized version of the normalized generator for a fair comparison. For 1,024 exponents from 17 through 2063, our serial CPU + GMP took 264 ms, and the 8-worker version took 46 ms: a 5.70x improvement. Generator + the same 8-worker pool took 64 ms, so the filter added a 1.39x gain at equal worker count. For 2,040 candidates, 12 workers gave a 6.40x improvement over our serial CPU and a 1.54x gain over the same-worker generator. All final classifications agreed, and each path performed its expected number of GMP tests on every warmup and timed call. Metal plus the pool did not improve the CPU median in these finite tests.

Your exact Crystal `primes-utils` implementation was unavailable, so these are comparisons using one common GMP probable-prime tester, not timings of your original backend. Raw samples, build commands, source hashes and exclusions are in [the multicore report](https://github.com/skuznetsov/mersenne-crystal-lab/blob/main/MULTICORE_COMPARISON.md) and [the direct-exponent report](https://github.com/skuznetsov/mersenne-crystal-lab/blob/main/EXPONENT_COMPARISON.md). Host load was uncontrolled, and these measured ratios are not universal speedups.

There is an important limit to this experiment: with these bounded trial factors, the order table cannot screen large prime exponents. A useful next step would be to compare against selecting prime exponents first, reproduce the finite results with your actual tester, and choose a suitable final Mersenne test. That would tell us whether the modular-group formulation offers an additional sound filter or another measurable contribution beyond standard screening.
